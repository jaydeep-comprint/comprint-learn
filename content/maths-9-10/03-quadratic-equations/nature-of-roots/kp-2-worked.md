## Worked example: nature of roots from D

**a)** $2x^2 - 3x + 5 = 0$: $D = 9 - 40 = -31 < 0$, so it has **no real roots**.

**b)** $9x^2 - 12x + 4 = 0$: $D = 144 - 144 = 0$, so it has **two equal real roots**.
Each root is $x = -\dfrac{b}{2a} = \dfrac{12}{18} = \dfrac{2}{3}$. (Check: $9x^2 - 12x + 4 = (3x - 2)^2$.)

**c)** $x^2 - 7x + 10 = 0$: $D = 49 - 40 = 9 > 0$, so it has **two distinct real roots**:
$x = \dfrac{7 \pm \sqrt{9}}{2} = \dfrac{7 \pm 3}{2}$, that is $x = 5$ or $x = 2$.

Equal roots count as **one distinct** root; "no real roots" means **zero** real roots.
