## Worked example: the value of a polynomial

Find $p(-2)$ for $p(x) = 3x^2 - 4x + 1$.

1. Replace every $x$ by $(-2)$, **in brackets**: $p(-2) = 3(-2)^2 - 4(-2) + 1$.
2. Powers first: $(-2)^2 = 4$, so the first term is $3 \times 4 = 12$.
3. Then $-4 \times (-2) = +8$.
4. Add: $p(-2) = 12 + 8 + 1 = 21$.

Two useful values: $p(0)$ is the constant term ($p(0) = 1$ here), and $p(1)$ is the sum of all the coefficients
($p(1) = 3 - 4 + 1 = 0$).
