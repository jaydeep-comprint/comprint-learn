# Momentum and the second law

The **momentum** of a moving object is its mass times its velocity:

**p = m × v**

Its SI unit is **kg m/s**. Momentum is a vector: it points the same way as the velocity. A heavy truck and a
fast cricket ball can both have a lot of momentum; a parked truck has none.

To change an object's momentum, a net force must act on it. The **second law of motion** says that the rate
of change of momentum is proportional to the net force, and happens in the direction of the force:

**F = (mv − mu) ÷ t = m(v − u) ÷ t**

Since (v − u) ÷ t is the acceleration, this is the same as **F = m × a**. One **newton** (1 N) is the force
that gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s², so 1 N = 1 kg m/s².

Always work in SI units: mass in kg, velocity in m/s, time in s.
