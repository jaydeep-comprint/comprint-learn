## Worked example: from one body's acceleration to the other's

A 60 kg sailor jumps from a 200 kg boat onto the shore. While his feet push off, he accelerates toward the
shore at 3 m/s². What is the boat's acceleration?

**Step 1: the force on the sailor (F = ma).** F = 60 × 3 = **180 N** toward the shore. This force comes from
the boat pushing on his feet.

**Step 2: the third law.** His feet push the boat with **180 N**, away from the shore.

**Step 3: the boat on its own (a = F ÷ m).** a = 180 ÷ 200 = **0.9 m/s²**, away from the shore.

Taking the direction toward the shore as positive, the boat's acceleration is −0.9 m/s².

**A shortcut to check.** The force is the same on both bodies, so m₁a₁ = m₂a₂:
60 × 3 = 200 × a, so a = 0.9 m/s².

**Adding other forces.** If there is also friction or water drag on a body, find the net force on that body
first. A swimmer whose hands push the water back with 80 N, against 30 N of drag, has a net forward force of
80 − 30 = 50 N; at 50 kg her acceleration is 50 ÷ 50 = 1 m/s².
