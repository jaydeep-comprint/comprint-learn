# Polynomials

An expression like $4x^3 - 2x^2 + x + 7$ is a **polynomial in $x$**: a sum of **terms**, each a number times a
whole-number power of $x$.

- In the term $-2x^2$, the number $-2$ is the **coefficient** (it keeps its sign). The term $7$ is the **constant term**.
- The **degree** is the highest power of $x$ in any term. $4x^3 - 2x^2 + x + 7$ has degree 3.
  Degree 1 is **linear**, 2 **quadratic**, 3 **cubic**; a non-zero constant like $7$ has degree 0.
- $p(a)$, the **value** of $p(x)$ at $x = a$, is what you get when you replace every $x$ by $a$.
- A **zero** of $p(x)$ is a number $a$ with $p(a) = 0$.

Expressions like $\dfrac{1}{x}$ or $\sqrt{x}$ are not polynomials: the powers of $x$ must be $0, 1, 2, \ldots$
