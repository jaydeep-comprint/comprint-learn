# Factorisation of polynomials

To **factorise** a polynomial is to write it as a product of simpler polynomials, called its **factors**:
$x^2 + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)$. Factors make the zeros easy to read: this polynomial is 0 when $x = -2$ or $x = -3$.

- **Factor theorem**: $x - a$ is a factor of $p(x)$ exactly when $p(a) = 0$. Watch the sign: $x + 3 = x - (-3)$,
  so $x + 3$ is a factor when $p(-3) = 0$.
- **Splitting the middle term**: for $ax^2 + bx + c$, find two numbers with product $ac$ and sum $b$.
- **Identities** in reverse: $x^2 - y^2 = (x - y)(x + y)$, $x^2 \pm 2xy + y^2 = (x \pm y)^2$, and the cube identities.
- **Cubics**: find one zero by trial, divide out its factor, then factorise the quadratic that is left.

You can always check a factorisation by multiplying the factors back out.
