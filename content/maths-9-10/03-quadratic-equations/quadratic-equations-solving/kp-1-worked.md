## Worked example: recognising a quadratic equation

**a)** Is $(x - 2)^2 + 1 = 2x - 3$ a quadratic equation? Expand and bring everything to the left:
$x^2 - 4x + 4 + 1 - 2x + 3 = 0$, so $x^2 - 6x + 8 = 0$. It has the form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ with $a = 1 \neq 0$: **yes**.

**b)** Is $x(x + 1) + 8 = (x + 2)(x - 2)$ quadratic? Expanding gives $x^2 + x + 8 = x^2 - 4$. The $x^2$ terms
cancel, leaving $x + 12 = 0$: **no**, it is linear. Always rearrange before deciding.

**c)** Read $a$, $b$ and $c$ from $3x^2 = 5x - 2$. First rearrange: $3x^2 - 5x + 2 = 0$. So $a = 3$, $b = -5$, $c = 2$
(not $b = 5$: the sign changes when $5x$ moves across).

**d)** Is $x = 2$ a root of $3x^2 - 5x + 2 = 0$? Substitute: $3(2)^2 - 5(2) + 2 = 12 - 10 + 2 = 4 \neq 0$: **no**.
Is $x = 1$? $3 - 5 + 2 = 0$: **yes**.
