# Equations of motion

When an object moves in a straight line with **uniform (constant) acceleration**, five quantities describe its
motion: the initial velocity **u**, the final velocity **v**, the acceleration **a**, the time **t** and the
displacement **s**. Three equations link them:

- $v = u + at$ (you met this one in Acceleration)
- $s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2$
- $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$

Each equation leaves out one quantity: the first has no $s$, the second no $v$, the third no $t$. To solve a
problem, list what you know and what you want, then pick the equation that contains exactly those.

They only work when the acceleration stays the same. Choose one direction as positive: a car that is slowing
down has an acceleration opposite to its velocity, so $a$ is negative.
