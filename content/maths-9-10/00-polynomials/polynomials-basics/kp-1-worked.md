## Worked example: terms, coefficients and degree

Look at $p(x) = 6x - 3x^5 + 2x^2 - 9$.

1. Its **terms** are $6x$, $-3x^4$, $2x^2$ and $-9$: four terms.
2. The **coefficient** of $x^2$ is $2$; the coefficient of $x^5$ is $-3$ (the sign belongs to the coefficient).
3. The **degree** is the highest power of $x$: the powers are 1, 5, 2 and 0, so the degree is **5**. (It has 4
   terms, but the number of terms is not the degree.) Writing it in order of power makes this easy to see:
   $p(x) = -3x^5 + 2x^2 + 6x - 9$.
4. If a coefficient can be zero, the term disappears: $(k - 2)x^3 + x^2$ is quadratic exactly when $k = 2$.
