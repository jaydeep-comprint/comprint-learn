## Worked example: choosing the right equation

| Equation | Has no |
|---|---|
| $v = u + at$ | $s$ |
| $s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2$ | $v$ |
| $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ | $t$ |

**Problem**: a train speeds up uniformly from 10 m/s to 20 m/s while covering 300 m. What is its acceleration,
and how long does it take?

1. **List the knowns**: u = 10 m/s, v = 20 m/s, s = 300 m. **Want**: a.
2. Time is neither known nor wanted, so use the equation with no $t$: $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$.
   $400 = 100 + 2 \times a \times 300$, so $a = 300 \div 600 =$ **0.5 m/s²**.
3. Now a is known too. For t, use $v = u + at$: $t = (20 - 10) \div 0.5 =$ **20 s**.

**Shortcut**: with uniform acceleration the average velocity is $\tfrac{u + v}{2}$, so
$s = \tfrac{u + v}{2} \times t$ (the trapezium area under the velocity–time graph). Here
300 = 15 × t gives t = 20 s directly.

Always convert units first (km/h → m/s by dividing by 3.6, minutes → seconds), and keep one direction positive.
