## Worked example: mixed units and two-step problems

**1. Braking in km/h.** A car of mass 1000 kg moving at 54 km/h stops in 5 s. What is the average braking force?

- Convert: 54 ÷ 3.6 = 15 m/s.
- Change in momentum = 1000 × (0 − 15) = −15 000 kg m/s.
- Force = −15 000 ÷ 5 = −3000 N: a braking force of **3000 N**, backwards.

**2. Grams.** A net force of 2 N acts on a 400 g toy car. Mass = 0.4 kg, so a = 2 ÷ 0.4 = **5 m/s²**.

**3. Force, then velocity.** A 20 kg trolley starts from rest. It is pushed with 50 N against 10 N of friction
for 4 s. How fast is it moving then?

- Net force = 50 − 10 = 40 N
- a = F ÷ m = 40 ÷ 20 = 2 m/s²
- v = u + at = 0 + 2 × 4 = **8 m/s**

**4. Bouncing back.** A 200 g hockey ball moving at 10 m/s is hit and moves straight back at 5 m/s. Take the
first direction as positive, so the final velocity is −5 m/s.

Change in momentum = 0.2 × (−5 − 10) = **−3 kg m/s**. Forgetting the sign (0.2 × (5 − 10) = −1) is a common slip:
reversing direction needs a bigger change than stopping.
