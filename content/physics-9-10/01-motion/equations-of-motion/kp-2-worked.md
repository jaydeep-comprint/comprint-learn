## Worked example: v² = u² + 2as

A car moving at 6 m/s accelerates uniformly at 2 m/s² over a distance of 16 m. What is its final velocity?

**Knowns**: u = 6 m/s, a = 2 m/s², s = 16 m. **Want**: v. No time is given, so use $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$.

$v^2 = 6^2 + 2 \times 2 \times 16 = 36 + 64 = 100$, so $v = \sqrt{100} =$ **10 m/s**.

Square u first, then add 2as, and take the square root only at the end ($\sqrt{36 + 64}$ is 10, not 6 + 8).

**Finding the distance instead**: rearrange to $s = \dfrac{v^2 - u^2}{2a}$. To speed up from 6 m/s to 10 m/s at
2 m/s², the car needs s = (100 − 36) ÷ (2 × 2) = 64 ÷ 4 = **16 m**.
