# Nature of roots

Before solving $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ ($a \neq 0$), you can find out **how many real roots** it has from one number,
the **discriminant**:

$$D = b^2 - 4ac$$

It is the number under the square root in the quadratic formula $x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a}$.

- $D > 0$: **two distinct real roots**.
- $D = 0$: **two equal real roots**, both $x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}$.
- $D < 0$: **no real roots** (there is no real square root of a negative number).

Read $a$, $b$ and $c$ only after writing the equation in standard form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$.
The discriminant also answers "is it possible?" questions: if the equation for a situation has no real roots,
the situation cannot happen.
