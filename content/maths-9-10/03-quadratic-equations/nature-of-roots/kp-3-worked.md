## Worked example: finding k

**a)** Find $k$ if $kx^2 - 6x + 1 = 0$ has two equal roots.

Equal roots means $D = 0$. Here $a = k$, $b = -6$, $c = 1$: $D = 36 - 4k = 0$, so $k = 9$.
(Check: $9x^2 - 6x + 1 = (3x - 1)^2$.)

**b)** Find the positive value of $k$ for which $4x^2 - kx + 9 = 0$ has equal roots.
$D = k^2 - 4 \times 4 \times 9 = k^2 - 144 = 0$, so $k = 12$ or $k = -12$. The positive value is $12$.

**c)** For which values of $k$ does $x^2 - 6x + k = 0$ have **real** roots?
Real roots means $D \geq 0$ (two distinct **or** two equal): $36 - 4k \geq 0$, so $k \leq 9$.
The greatest such $k$ is $9$; for $k > 9$ there are no real roots.
