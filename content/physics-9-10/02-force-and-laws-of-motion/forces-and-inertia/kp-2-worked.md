## Worked example: friction and constant velocity

Two children push a box across the floor, with 30 N and 25 N in the same direction. The box slides at a
**constant velocity**. What is the friction on it?

1. Constant velocity means the velocity is not changing: the acceleration is zero.
2. By Newton's first law, the motion changes only if there is an unbalanced force. So the forces are **balanced**.
3. The pushes add up to 30 + 25 = 55 N forwards, so friction must be **55 N backwards**.

**What if the push changes?** If the total push rises to 70 N and friction stays 55 N, the net force is
70 − 55 = 15 N forwards: the box speeds up. If the children stop pushing, only friction acts: the net force is
−55 N (taking forwards as positive), and the box slows down and stops.

**At rest is balanced too**: if you push a heavy cupboard with 200 N and it doesn't move, friction from the floor
is pushing back with 200 N.

A moving object doesn't need a force to keep moving. Things on Earth stop because friction (or air resistance)
acts on them. On very smooth ice, with little friction, a puck slides a long way.
