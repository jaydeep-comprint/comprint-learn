## Worked example: the quadratic formula

Solve $2x^2 + 1 = 4x$. Give the roots to 2 decimal places.

1. Standard form first: $2x^2 - 4x + 1 = 0$, so $a = 2$, $b = -4$, $c = 1$.
2. $b^2 - 4ac = (-4)^2 - 4(2)(1) = 16 - 8 = 8$. It is not negative, so there are real roots.
3. $x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} = \dfrac{-(-4) \pm \sqrt{8}}{2 \times 2} = \dfrac{4 \pm \sqrt{8}}{4}$.
   Here $-b = +4$, because $b$ is negative.
4. $\sqrt{8} \approx 2.828$, so $x \approx \dfrac{6.828}{4} \approx 1.71$ or $x \approx \dfrac{1.172}{4} \approx 0.29$.

The formula also works when the equation factorises: for $x^2 - x - 6 = 0$, $b^2 - 4ac = 1 + 24 = 25$ and
$x = \dfrac{1 \pm 5}{2}$, so $x = 3$ or $x = -2$. If $b^2 - 4ac$ is negative, there are no real roots.
