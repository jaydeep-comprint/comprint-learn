## Worked example: zeros of a polynomial

A **zero** of $p(x)$ is a value of $x$ that makes $p(x) = 0$.

**a)** Find the zero of $p(x) = 2x - 5$. Solve $2x - 5 = 0$: $2x = 5$, so $x = 2.5$.
Check: $p(2.5) = 5 - 5 = 0$. ✓

**b)** Is $x = 3$ a zero of $q(x) = x^2 - 2x - 3$? $q(3) = 9 - 6 - 3 = 0$, so yes.

**c)** $x = 2$ is a zero of $r(x) = x^2 + kx - 6$. Find $k$. Put $x = 2$ and set it equal to 0:
$4 + 2k - 6 = 0$, so $2k = 2$ and $k = 1$.

Don't mix up a zero with $p(0)$: for $p(x) = x + 5$, $p(0) = 5$, but the zero is $x = -5$.
A non-zero constant polynomial, like $p(x) = 7$, has no zeros.
