## Worked example: force as rate of change of momentum

The second law: **F = m(v − u) ÷ t**, or **F × t = m(v − u) = change in momentum**.

**Speeding up.** A net force speeds a 4 kg cart from 2 m/s to 8 m/s in 3 s.

- Change in momentum = m(v − u) = 4 × (8 − 2) = 24 kg m/s
- F = 24 ÷ 3 = **8 N**

**Stopping a ball.** A fielder catches a 150 g cricket ball moving at 20 m/s. Take the ball's direction as
positive. Mass = 0.15 kg.

- Change in momentum = 0.15 × (0 − 20) = **−3 kg m/s**. The minus sign means the change points backwards,
  against the motion.
- If the hands stop the ball in 0.02 s, the average force = 3 ÷ 0.02 = **150 N**.
- If she pulls her hands back and stops it in 0.2 s, the force = 3 ÷ 0.2 = **15 N**.

The change in momentum is the same either way (the ball always goes from 20 m/s to rest). Pulling the hands
back makes the time 10 times longer, so the force on her hands is 10 times smaller. Cushions, sand pits for
the high jump and crumple zones in cars work the same way.

**Finding the time or velocity.** F × t = change in momentum. A 10 N net force acting on a 5 kg cart at rest
for 2 s gives it momentum 10 × 2 = 20 kg m/s, so its velocity is 20 ÷ 5 = 4 m/s.
