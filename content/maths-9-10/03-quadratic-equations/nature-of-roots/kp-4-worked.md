## Worked example: is it possible?

Is it possible to design a rectangular park of perimeter 60 m and area 250 m²? If so, find its length and breadth.

1. Half the perimeter is $30$ m, so if the length is $x$ m, the breadth is $(30 - x)$ m.
2. Area: $x(30 - x) = 250$, so $30x - x^2 = 250$, which in standard form is $x^2 - 30x + 250 = 0$.
3. $D = (-30)^2 - 4 \times 1 \times 250 = 900 - 1000 = -100 < 0$.
4. There are no real roots, so **no such park is possible**.

With area 225 m² instead: $x^2 - 30x + 225 = 0$ gives $D = 900 - 900 = 0$, so it is possible, with equal roots
$x = 15$: a square park, 15 m by 15 m. In general $D \geq 0$ needs $900 - 4A \geq 0$, so 225 m² is the
greatest area a 60 m perimeter can enclose.
