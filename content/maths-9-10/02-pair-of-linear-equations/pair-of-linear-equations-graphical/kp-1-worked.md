## Worked example: the ratio test

Without drawing, decide how the lines $2x + 3y = 9$ and $4x + 6y = 18$ are related.

1. Write both as $ax + by + c = 0$: $2x + 3y - 9 = 0$ and $4x + 6y - 18 = 0$.
2. Ratios: $\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$, $\frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$, $\frac{c_1}{c_2} = \frac{-9}{-18} = \frac{1}{2}$.
3. All three are equal, so the lines are **coincident**: infinitely many solutions.

If only the first two ratios had been equal, the lines would be **parallel** (no solution). If the first two differ, the lines **intersect** in exactly one point. Equivalently, the lines intersect exactly when $a_1b_2 - a_2b_1 \ne 0$.
