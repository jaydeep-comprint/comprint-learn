## Worked example: solving by factorisation

Solve $2x^2 - 5x + 3 = 0$.

1. Split the middle term: find two numbers with product $a \times c = 2 \times 3 = 6$ and sum $b = -5$.
   They are $-2$ and $-3$.
2. Rewrite and group: $2x^2 - 2x - 3x + 3 = 2x(x - 1) - 3(x - 1) = (2x - 3)(x - 1)$.
3. A product is 0 only when a factor is 0: $2x - 3 = 0$ or $x - 1 = 0$.
4. So $x = \dfrac{3}{2} = 1.5$ or $x = 1$.

Check: $2(1)^2 - 5(1) + 3 = 0$. ✓

When there is no constant term, take out $x$ as a factor; don't divide by it.
$x^2 = 5x$ gives $x^2 - 5x = 0$, so $x(x - 5) = 0$ and $x = 0$ **or** $x = 5$. Dividing by $x$ would lose the root 0.
