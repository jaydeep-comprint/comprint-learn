## Worked example: the discriminant

**a)** Find the discriminant of $2x^2 - 7x + 3 = 0$.

Here $a = 2$, $b = -7$, $c = 3$. Then $D = b^2 - 4ac = (-7)^2 - 4 \times 2 \times 3 = 49 - 24 = 25$.
$b^2$ is never negative: $(-7)^2 = 49$.

**b)** Find the discriminant of $3x^2 = 5x + 2$.

First rearrange into standard form: $3x^2 - 5x - 2 = 0$, so $a = 3$, $b = -5$, $c = -2$.
$D = (-5)^2 - 4 \times 3 \times (-2) = 25 + 24 = 49$. When $a$ and $c$ have opposite signs, $-4ac$ is positive.

**c)** Find the discriminant of $x^2 - 16 = 0$. There is no $x$ term, so $b = 0$:
$D = 0 - 4 \times 1 \times (-16) = 64$.
