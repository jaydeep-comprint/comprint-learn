## Worked example: (x + y + z)²

$(x + y + z)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + 2xy + 2yz + 2zx$: the square of each term, plus **twice every pair**.

**a)** Expand $(2a + b - 3)^2$. The three terms are $2a$, $b$ and $-3$.

1. Squares: $(2a)^2 = 4a^2$, $b^2$, $(-3)^2 = 9$.
2. Pairs, twice: $2(2a)(b) = 4ab$, $2(b)(-3) = -6b$, $2(-3)(2a) = -12a$.
3. So $(2a + b - 3)^2 = 4a^2 + b^2 + 9 + 4ab - 6b - 12a$.

A pair product is negative when exactly one of its two terms is negative.

**b)** If $a + b + c = 9$ and $ab + bc + ca = 26$, find $a^2 + b^2 + c^2$.
From the identity, $a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = (a + b + c)^2 - 2(ab + bc + ca) = 81 - 52 = 29$.
