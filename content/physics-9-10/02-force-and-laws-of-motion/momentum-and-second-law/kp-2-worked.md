## Worked example: F = ma

The second law gives **F = m × a**, where F is the **net** (resultant) force on the object.

**Finding the force.** What net force gives a 1200 kg car an acceleration of 2 m/s²?

F = m × a = 1200 × 2 = **2400 N**

**Finding the acceleration.** A net force of 30 N acts on a 6 kg trolley: a = F ÷ m = 30 ÷ 6 = **5 m/s²**.

**Finding the mass.** A net force of 12 N gives a box an acceleration of 4 m/s²: m = F ÷ a = 12 ÷ 4 = **3 kg**.
(Not a ÷ F: check that the units come out as kg.)

**Net force.** A worker pushes a 50 kg crate with 120 N. Friction pulls back with 20 N.

- Net force = 120 − 20 = 100 N, forwards.
- a = 100 ÷ 50 = **2 m/s²**.

If the push only just balances friction, the net force is zero, so a = 0: the crate moves at a constant
velocity (or stays at rest). No net force is needed to *keep* something moving.

**Units.** 1 N = 1 kg m/s². Mass must be in kg: a 250 g ball is 0.25 kg.
