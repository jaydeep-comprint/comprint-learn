## Worked example: why the pair doesn't cancel

A horse pulls a 400 kg cart forward with a force of 1000 N. Friction from the road on the cart is 600 N.
The cart pulls back on the horse with 1000 N. Can the cart move?

**Step 1: pick ONE body and list only the forces on it.** Take the cart.

- The horse pulls the cart forward: 1000 N.
- Friction pulls the cart backward: 600 N.

The cart's pull on the horse is **not** on this list: it acts on the horse.

**Step 2: add the forces on that body.**
Net force on the cart = 1000 − 600 = **400 N** forward.

**Step 3: use a = F ÷ m.** a = 400 ÷ 400 = **1 m/s²**. The cart speeds up.

The horse is a different body. Its forces are the ground pushing its hooves forward and the cart pulling it
back. If the ground's push is bigger than 1000 N, the horse speeds up too.

**Balanced forces are different.** A book rests on a table. Two forces act on the *book*: the Earth pulls it
down (its weight) and the table pushes it up. They are equal and opposite and they *do* cancel, because they
act on the same body. They are **not** a third-law pair: they are different kinds of force, and the partner of
the Earth's pull on the book is the book's pull on the Earth.
