## Worked example: stopping distance

A driver is moving at 72 km/h when she sees a cow on the road. Her reaction time is 0.5 s, and then the
brakes give a steady retardation of 5 m/s². How far does the car go before it stops?

1. **Convert**: 72 km/h ÷ 3.6 = 20 m/s.
2. **Reaction distance**: before she presses the brake, the car keeps moving at 20 m/s:
   20 × 0.5 = **10 m**.
3. **Braking distance**: u = 20 m/s, v = 0, a = −5 m/s² (the direction of motion is positive, so the
   retardation is negative). No time is needed, so use $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$:
   $0 = 20^2 + 2 \times (-5) \times s$, so $s = 400 \div 10 =$ **40 m**.
4. **Stopping distance** = 10 + 40 = **50 m**.

**Time to stop** once braking starts: $v = u + at$ gives $0 = 20 - 5t$, so t = **4 s**.

Braking distance is $\dfrac{u^2}{2 \times \text{retardation}}$: it depends on the *square* of the speed.
At double the speed (40 m/s) the braking distance is 4 times as long: 160 m.
