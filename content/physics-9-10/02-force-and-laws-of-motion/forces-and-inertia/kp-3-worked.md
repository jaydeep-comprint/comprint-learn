## Worked example: comparing inertia

A cricket ball has a mass of 160 g and a shot-put ball has a mass of 4 kg. Which has more inertia, and how
many times as much?

1. **Mass is the measure of inertia**, so compare the masses.
2. Put them in the same unit: 4 kg = 4000 g.
3. 4000 ÷ 160 = **25**. The shot put has 25 times as much inertia: it is much harder to start moving, to stop
   and to turn. That is why catching a shot put would hurt far more than catching a cricket ball.

Mass doesn't depend on where the object is. A 4 kg shot put still has a mass of 4 kg, and the same inertia,
on the Moon or floating in a spacecraft.

**Inertia in everyday life**

- **A bus brakes suddenly**: passengers lurch forwards, because their bodies tend to keep moving (inertia of motion).
- **A bus starts suddenly**: passengers fall backwards, because their bodies tend to stay at rest (inertia of rest).
- **Shaking a branch**: the branch moves quickly, but the fruit tends to stay where it is, so it comes off.
- **Coin on a card**: flick a card off a glass and the coin on top drops into the glass, because it stays at rest.
